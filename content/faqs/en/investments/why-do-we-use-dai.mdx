Due to the high volatility of Ether, investors feared that their returns would be diminished. The uncertainty of where ETH would trade made investors prefer something more stable.

That is why at EthicHub we turned to this stablecoin, implementing it for investing in projects since the beginning of January 2020.

Behind this stablecoin there is no centralized authority, nor a financial institution issuing these assets, as is the case with other cryptocurrencies like Tether.

DAI are tokens (implementations) on the Ethereum network, a decentralized platform that enables the creation of smart contracts on a P2P network, which also incorporates a cryptocurrency called Ether (ETH). DAI are backed by ETH and thus obtain a stability that other crypto assets do not have. This means DAI does not lose value and better withstands severe market fluctuations.

Due to its decentralized nature, it is resistant to censorship as no entity has full control over the network. This quality is highly valued by those who need to send remittances to other countries or structure deferred payments over time.

Through an incentive system, DAI tends asymptotically toward a value of $1 USD. When DAI is worth more than $1, ETH holders will seek to create more DAI to sell it and obtain a profit. Conversely, when DAI is worth less than $1, those who need to recover the ETH they used to mint DAI previously will seek to buy DAI at a cheaper price, driving the price back to $1 through combined demand.

DAI is an ERC20 token whose price tracks the USD. This token "lives" on the decentralized blockchain called Ethereum, whose native currency is Ether (ETH).

Ethereum's security is based on a series of computers competing to validate transactions made on the network so that they become part of the common ledger.

The competition process to reach consensus on which transactions are valid on Ethereum is called Proof of Work. The participating computers (validator nodes or miners) are rewarded for performing this work (in ETH), and the more the network is used, the more rewards they earn. Being a decentralized network, it is the network users who bear the costs every time a transaction is submitted.

A few years ago, the R&D company [POA](https://www.poa.network/) created xDAI, a blockchain network very similar to Ethereum (the same addresses and smart contracts are used) but whose native currency, instead of being Ether, is xDAI, a token that always has the same value as DAI.

The consensus mechanism of xDAI is Proof of Authority, which is less costly for validator nodes, resulting in very low network fees that are also paid in xDAI (just cents of USD). Since there is no bidding to include transactions, confirmation times are shorter.

These advantages come at the cost of sacrificing degrees of decentralization. There are fewer nodes validating transactions, but their identity is legally known, so they are incentivized to act honestly.

To generate xDAI, a user must send DAI to a special smart contract deployed on Ethereum called TokenBridge, which will lock the DAI on Ethereum and communicate with its counterpart on xDAI to create the same number of xDAI tokens and send them to the user's address, thus connecting the networks. The reverse mechanism is similar (from xDAI to DAI).

xDAI as a currency is therefore the representation of DAI on the xDAI blockchain.
